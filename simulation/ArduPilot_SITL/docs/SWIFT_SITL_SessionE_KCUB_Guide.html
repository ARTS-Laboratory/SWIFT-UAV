Out-and-back range test from Owens Downtown (KCUB)

ArduPlane flies the swift_6dof_sitl Simulink model on one long straight leg out along the runway 13 heading, one gentle right 180°, and the same leg back, at the airspeed that gives the most distance per unit of charge. No other manoeuvres. The turn-back point is set so the model lands with about 3 Ah left. The same flight is the first full check of the inverted-V tail in SITL, and it exercises every plot plus the flight video.

SPDWhy 22.5 m/s is the most efficient setting

ArduPlane in AUTO holds airspeed, not throttle: the total energy control system (TECS) sets whatever throttle that takes. So "most efficient throttle" means flying the most efficient airspeed and reading the throttle it needs.

Energy per km against airspeed (model, level flight)

Throttle that holds that airspeed (MAD X7215 table)

How swift_sitl_cub_e picks it

Start from the level-flight drag (thrust = drag):

T(V) = D(V) = q S ( CD0 + k CL2 ),   CL = W / (q S),   q = ½ ρ V2
  • T thrust (N), D drag (N), V true airspeed (m/s), q dynamic pressure (Pa), S = 0.81 m² wing area, ρ = 1.225 kg/m³ air density, W = 209.7 N weight at 21.38 kg, CD0 = 0.035 parasite drag coefficient, k = 0.0477 induced-drag factor, CL lift coefficient (–)

The pack power for that thrust comes from momentum theory and the motor efficiency (the same equations as swift_battery_fcn), and the range per unit of energy is airspeed over power:

Ppack = T(V + vi) / (ηprop ηmot) + Photel,   E/s = Ppack / V  →  minimum over V
  • Ppack power out of the pack (W), vi propeller induced velocity (m/s), ηprop = 0.65 propeller efficiency, ηmot = 0.82 motor + electronic speed controller (ESC) efficiency, Photel ≈ 9 W avionics, E/s energy per distance (J/m; ÷ 3.6 → Wh/km)

At 22.5 m/s: Ppack ≈ 775 W, 17.0 A, 9.56 Wh/km, 4.77 km per Ah. The curve is very flat between 22 and 24 m/s, and the unconstrained optimum sits close to the 22 m/s AIRSPEED_MIN floor (1.22 × stall), so the exact pick matters less than flying it steadily. The sweep is rerun from your current P every time, so a change in mass or drag moves the speed automatically. swift_sitl_cub_e('v_range', 24) forces another speed for a comparison flight.

VTWhat this flight checks on the V-tail

ArduPlane sends two V-tail outputs (SERVO2 function 79, SERVO4 function 80, MIXING_GAIN 1). The model decodes them back to elevator and rudder, limits each ruddervator at ±25°, and logs both as out_rv. swift_vtail_check grades six things after the flight.

Inverted V, as built

The mixing and the six checks

rvL = δe + δr,   rvR = δe − δr,   |rv| ≤ 25°
  • rvL, rvR left and right ruddervator angles (deg, + = trailing edge down), δe equivalent elevator (deg), δr equivalent rudder (deg)
LOGGEDout_rv is in the run MIXINGelevator and rudder flown = un-mix of the two surfaces (< 0.05°) TRAVELon a stop < 1 % of airborne time PITCH SIGNboth surfaces trailing edge up at takeoff rotation YAW SIGNrudder goes into the turn with the ailerons (the right 180° and the pattern turns) STRAIGHTon the long legs both surfaces sit together (|δr| < 1°)

P1MATLAB: set up and start the model~3 min

Order matters. swift_sitl_preflight rebuilds P and parks the aircraft facing north, so swift_sitl_cub_e must run after it to turn the aircraft onto runway 13.

Set up the folder and run the preflight MATLAB

  1. Open MATLAB and click in the Command Window.
  2. Copy the block below, paste it with Ctrl+V, and press Enter.
cd('<repo>\simulation\ArduPilot_SITL')
swift_setup
swift_sitl_preflight('ADDR')
swift_setup should print ARDUPILOT_SITL is ACTIVE. The parameter summary should list the V-tail table (Ruddervators +/- 25 deg each) and the propulsion line MAD X7215 IPE 220KV, 145 N static.

Pick the speed and turn-back point, write the files MATLAB

Parks the aircraft on runway 13, checks the battery and V-tail pieces, sweeps airspeed for the best range, solves the turn-back point for about 3 Ah left at touchdown, writes the mission and parameter files, and prints the plan and the Ubuntu commands with your address filled in. Takes about 10 s.

S = swift_sitl_cub_e('addr', 'ADDR');
Look for [ OK ] on every line under BATTERY AND V-TAIL CHECK (including logs out_rv), the best-range table with <- fly on the 22.5 m/s row, Turn back at x = 14.7 km, about 33.4 km and 25.7 min, Lands with ~2994 mAh left and P.sitl.hdg0 = 125.55 deg.

Want a shorter test? S = swift_sitl_cub_e('addr', '<addr>', 'x_turn', 5000); turns back at 5 km. To land with more in reserve, raise 'land_mAh' (default 3000).

Open the model and press Run Simulink

  1. Paste the line below into the Command Window and press Enter.
  2. On the Simulation tab, check Stop Time reads inf (this flight is about 26 min).
  3. Click the green Run button.
open_system('swift_6dof_sitl')

The clock stays at 0 until ArduPlane connects. You have about 120 s to start P3.

After this step, don't run swift_placeholder, clear or swift_sitl_preflight: each one resets the heading to north and the battery to its defaults. Keep S in the workspace, since the plot scripts read it.

Connect Mission Planner and add the V-tail and power readouts (optional) Mission Planner

Range check MATLAB New

Finds the steady cruise legs, prints a table (time, km, airspeed, groundspeed, throttle, current, mAh/km, km/Ah, Wh/km), compares the charge used with the plan, and projects the still-air range to the 2000 mAh reserve. Saves swift_range_check.png, range_summary.txt and range_legs.csv.

RC = swift_range_check(outE, 'tag', 'sessionE_cub');
Expect two main legs named out and back (a short back 2 may appear on the level part of the descent), both near 22.5 m/s and ~53 % throttle, about 9.56 Wh/km on both. In mAh/km the back leg reads a few percent worse because the pack voltage is lower; Wh/km is the fair comparison. Flight versus plan should be within ±5 %.

V-tail check MATLAB New

Grades the six checks in the V-tail section and draws the ruddervators against time, the elevator/rudder identity, the travel envelope, yaw coordination and the pitch trim against the model. Saves swift_vtail_check.png and vtail_summary.txt.

VT = swift_vtail_check(outE, 'tag', 'sessionE_cub');
The figure title should read ALL CHECKS PASS. Mixing error should be ~0.000°, travel used well under 50 % after takeoff, and the yaw-coordination points in the two green quadrants.

Flight video MATLAB

At 20× speed the ~26 min flight is about 77 s of video (~2300 frames). Rendering takes several minutes; the Command Window counts the frames. Leave the window alone while it records.

swift_flight_video(outE, 'tag', 'sessionE_cub', 'speed', 20);

Check a few moments first if you like. This writes PNG stills to video_frames\ in a few seconds: the climb, the middle of the out leg, the turn back, and short final. The times come from the numbered mission items, because the log also holds the wait before AUTO.

E  = R.events;  tt = @(n) E.t(strcmp(E.item, n)) / 60;   % minutes into the log
at = [tt('#1'), (tt('#3') + tt('#5'))/2, tt('#7'), tt('#15')];
swift_flight_video(outE, 'tag', 'sessionE_cub', 'at', at);
The aircraft is drawn as SWIFT: red pod and wing, twin booms, an inverted V with cyan ruddervators that move as flown, and a pusher prop. The V-TAIL inset in the lower left of the 3D view shows a rear view and side sections of both ruddervators with their angles. They go green, then amber past 15°, then red at the 25° stop.
